Here is a sample visualization.

We have an infinite canvas where we'll show the app as root and all the apps and packages and leaves. We will provide the user a slider at the bottom. Something similar to what internet archives display so that they can select a slice in time. Let's say 2 weeks in october they were on vacation. This timeline will be built using the git repo's history. It'll inform us about the whole life of the repo.

To improve the QOL, the system will only highlight the apps or packages touched during that duration. It will disable the rest and gray them out to reduce visual information overload.

The owner can then quickly verify on level 1 whether any change was made in a domain relevant to them. In this case the render engine.

Once they expand it in level 2, we will reveal more information gradually. This time the leaves under the render engine will display the cluster label of all the changes that were made in which this package was involved (directly or indirectly).

Once they click on any of those changes, we will show them a hollistic picture of how that change was built in level 3.
